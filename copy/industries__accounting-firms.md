## 2. Where the hours go

Every quarter, someone assembles tax letters client by client — pulling the numbers, fitting them into the firm's format, checking names against the file. The work is identical across clients, and it still gets done one at a time.

Before every deadline, someone chases documents: the same reminder, personalized by hand, sent to each client who hasn't uploaded. Status questions arrive all week, and each one means opening TaxDome to look.

## 3. What I build for accounting firms

### Quarterly tax-letter workflow

An automation that assembles each client's quarterly letter — data pulled from your books, the letter drafted by AI in your format, queued for a reviewer before anything goes out. The reviewer approves or edits; the system sends and logs.

**Tools:** QuickBooks Online, TaxDome, OpenAI or Anthropic, Gmail

### Document chasing and deadline communications

An automation that watches which clients haven't submitted, drafts each reminder from that client's actual status, and escalates as the deadline approaches. Nothing sends without the review step your firm sets.

**Tools:** TaxDome, Gmail, OpenAI

### Bookkeeping workflows in QuickBooks Online

Automations that create customers and invoices, move data between systems, and keep the books in sync with the tools around them. QuickBooks invoice creation already runs in my production work — the Dahlia+ Agency pipeline creates the customer and two invoices the moment a proposal is accepted.

**Tools:** QuickBooks Online, plus whatever feeds it

### Client intake and status

Intake that creates the client record everywhere it needs to exist, and status updates drafted automatically when work moves stages. The inbox stops being the status system.

**Tools:** TaxDome, Gmail, your CRM
