## 2. Where the hours go

At a small agency, new business arrives from everywhere — web forms, referral emails, business cards collected at events — and someone retypes each one into the CRM. The ones that don't get retyped don't get followed up.

An accepted proposal kicks off an hour and a half of administration: update the deal stage, build the project board, create the customer in QuickBooks, draft the invoices. At Dahlia+ Agency's pace of two to four proposals a day, that was six to eight hours of manual operations every working day.

## 3. What I build — the four automations at Dahlia+ Agency

### Proposal-to-invoice pipeline

When a proposal is accepted in Fresh Proposals, the automation updates the HubSpot deal stage, builds the Monday.com board, and creates the QuickBooks customer with two invoices on a 50/50 split — untouched. Processing went from 1.5–2 hours per proposal to fifteen minutes.

**Tools:** Fresh Proposals, HubSpot, Monday.com, QuickBooks

### Web form lead routing

A form submission becomes a HubSpot contact, joins the Beehiiv list, and posts an alert in Slack. Every lead lands in the same place, logged the moment it arrives.

**Tools:** HubSpot, Beehiiv, Slack

### AI referral capture

An AI parses referral emails as they arrive and creates the HubSpot record — introductions get logged the day they happen. The follow-up starts from the CRM instead of from memory.

**Tools:** Gmail, OpenAI, HubSpot

### Business card scanner

Drop a photo in Slack; an AI reads the card, extracts every field, and creates the HubSpot contact. The same workflow running at Dahlia+ is in the template catalog for $249.

**Tools:** Slack, OpenAI GPT-4o Vision, HubSpot

### Reporting (capability line, below the four)

Client and performance reporting is the same kind of build. At Matador Network, a Monday report ranks the top twenty affiliate links from GA4 with AI commentary — the agency version reports on your clients' numbers instead of your own.
